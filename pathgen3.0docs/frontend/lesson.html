Lesson 1 of 3

Understanding and representing integers

Learn to identify integers, understand opposites and absolute value, and place any integer on a number line.

Skills S1 integer-identification S5 number-line

What you'll be able to do

  1. Identify integers, opposites, and absolute value.
  2. Represent, compare, and order integers on a number line.

What is an integer?

An integer is a whole number. It can be positive, negative, or zero. It has no fractional part and no decimal.

  • Integers: −8, −3, 0, 5, 12
  • Not integers: 3/4, 0.6, 12%

We use negative numbers every day. A temperature 3 degrees below zero is −3. A debt of 500 pesos is −500. A diver 6 meters under the sea is at −6.

The number line

A number line shows integers in order. Zero is in the middle. Positive integers go right. Negative integers go left.

Values increase as you move right. So −7 < −2 < 0 < 4.

Compare by position, not by digit size. −7 is smaller than −2 because it sits farther to the left.

Opposites and absolute value

Opposites are two integers the same distance from zero on different sides. −5 and 5 are opposites.

Absolute value is the distance from zero. Distance is always positive or zero. We write it with two bars.

|5| = 5 |−5| = 5 |0| = 0

Worked example

Worked example

Is −11 an integer? What is its opposite? What is |−11|?

  1. −11 has no fraction and no decimal. So it is an integer.
  2. The opposite of −11 is 11. Same distance, other side of zero. opposite(−11) = 11
  3. |−11| = 11 because −11 is 11 units from zero. |−11| = 11
Answer: −11 is an integer. Opposite: 11. Absolute value: 11.

Common mistake

Common mistake

−7 > −3 is wrong. The digit 7 is bigger, but −7 is farther left. Correct: −7 < −3.

Lesson 2 of 3

Operations with integers

Add, subtract, multiply, and divide integers using zero pairs and the sign rules that govern every operation.

Skills S2 add-subtract S3 multiply-divide

What you'll be able to do

  1. Add and subtract integers.
  2. Multiply and divide integers.

Zero pairs

When you add integers with different signs, a positive chip and a negative chip cancel. Each pair equals zero.

Example: −5 + 3. Start with 5 negative chips. Add 3 positive chips. Three pairs cancel. Two negatives remain.

−5 + 3 = −2

Adding same signs

Same signs: add the distances and keep the sign.

(−4) + (−3) = −7 4 + 3 = 7

Subtracting

Subtracting an integer is the same as adding its opposite.

a − b = a + (−b)

Example: 4 − (−3) = 4 + 3 = 7.

Multiplying and dividing

Work with distances first. Then decide the sign.

  • Same signs → positive
  • Different signs → negative
(−6) × 4 = −24 (−6) × (−4) = 24 (−24) ÷ (−6) = 4 24 ÷ (−6) = −4

Worked example

Worked example

Compute (−6) × 4 and (−24) ÷ (−6).

  1. Signs of (−6) × 4 are different. Result is negative. 6 × 4 = 24. Answer 1: −24
  2. Signs of (−24) ÷ (−6) are the same. Result is positive. 24 ÷ 6 = 4. Answer 2: 4
Answers: (−6) × 4 = −24 and (−24) ÷ (−6) = 4.

Common mistake

Common mistake

(−6) × 4 = 24 is wrong. The signs are different, so the answer is negative. Correct: (−6) × 4 = −24.

Lesson 3 of 3

Application of integers

Evaluate expressions using order of operations and model real-world situations where quantities rise and fall.

Skills S4 order-of-operations S6 signed-change

What you'll be able to do

  1. Evaluate expressions using order of operations.
  2. Model signed-change situations.

A story about temperature

The temperature at 7:00 AM was −7 °C. By noon it rose by 8 degrees.

The change is +8. Add: −7 + 8 = 1. The new temperature is 1 °C.

Order of operations

When an expression has more than one operation, always work in this order:

  1. Grouping first: ( ), [ ], { }.
  2. Multiply and divide, left to right.
  3. Add and subtract, left to right.

Words that mean signs

  • Rise, gain, deposit, up → positive
  • Fall, loss, withdrawal, down → negative

Interpreting the result

Always include the unit. Say if the result is positive or negative. Explain what it means.

Worked example

Worked example

Evaluate −3 × (−4) + (−18) ÷ 6 − (−5).

  1. Do multiplication and division first, left to right. −3 × (−4) = 12 (−18) ÷ 6 = −3
  2. Rewrite the expression. 12 + (−3) − (−5)
  3. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive. 12 + (−3) + 5
  4. Add left to right. 12 − 3 = 9, then 9 + 5 = 14
Answer: 14.

Common mistake

Common mistake

Adding before dividing is wrong. In −3 × (−4) + (−18) ÷ 6, the division goes before the addition. Following the correct order gives 14.
